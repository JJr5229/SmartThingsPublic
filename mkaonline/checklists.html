These checklists summarize the application process as we have seen it work. State fees and forms change — always confirm current amounts and requirements with the Minnesota Department of Health or the Department of Human Services before you submit, or ask us to review your application first.

Checklist 01

Basic Home Care License

For providers assisting with most activities of daily living in a person’s own home, where the person cannot perform those tasks themselves because of illness, disability or physical condition. A nurse is not required on the application.

  • Register your business with the Minnesota Secretary of State.
  • Obtain a Federal Tax ID from the IRS and a State Tax ID from the Minnesota Department of Revenue.
  • Secure liability insurance and workers’ compensation insurance.
  • Complete the MDH application forms and pay the current application fee.
  • Put your policies and procedures manual in place before you apply.
  • Prepare the client and personnel forms you will be required to maintain.
  • Background studies are completed on all owners and managers once the application is received.
  • Expect the license to be issued within ninety days of a completed application.
  • Notify MDH within five days after admitting your first client.

Checklist 02

Comprehensive Home Care License

Required for skilled home health services — nursing, assessments, delegation, medication administration and therapies. This is also the license Medicare certification is built on.

  • Obtain liability insurance, workers’ compensation insurance, a fidelity bond and a surety bond.
  • Pay the current application fee (confirm the amount with MDH — fees change).
  • Complete the application forms. A registered nurse must be named on the application.
  • Send the required licensing fee, made payable to the MN Department of Health, with the completed application.
  • Background studies are completed on all owners and managers once the application is received.
  • The license is issued within ninety days of a completed application.
  • Notify the MN Department of Health within five days after admitting your first client.

Two things that catch new agencies

  • Your new license is temporary for one year. It becomes permanent only after a successful compliance survey.
  • You cannot apply for Medicare certification while your license is temporary. Build that year into your business plan.

Checklist 03

Assisted Living Facility License

Since August 2021, Minnesota licenses the facility and its services together under a single assisted living license administered by MDH. Housing with Services registration no longer exists.

  • Secure the house first. The address of the house becomes the address on the license and cannot be changed once the application is submitted.
  • Check the bedroom count. If the setting has fewer than five bedrooms, you cannot take clients on a CADI or BI waiver.
  • Submit the required licensing check, made payable as directed by MDH (confirm the current amount).
  • Provide the name, home address, phone, email and a copy of the license for your Assisted Living Director and your Nursing Supervisor.
  • Provide an organization chart and a written description of the business.
  • Provide a certificate of liability for workers’ compensation insurance.
  • Provide a certificate of liability for general liability insurance.
  • Plan for the Licensed Assisted Living Director requirement — qualifying coursework up front, plus continuing training every two years.

Checklist 04

245D Home & Community-Based Services License

Licensed by the Department of Human Services rather than MDH, 245D governs basic and intensive support services for people receiving waiver funding — people with disabilities and people over sixty-five.

  • Register your business with the Minnesota Secretary of State.
  • Obtain a Federal Tax ID from the IRS and a State Tax ID from the Minnesota Department of Revenue.
  • Secure workers’ compensation insurance.
  • Apply for the 245D license from DHS online — or, once these initial steps are complete, contact MKA Consulting and we can apply on your behalf.
  • Pay the current license and provider enrollment fees (confirm amounts with DHS).
  • After approval, secure liability insurance.
  • Complete Waiver 101 Training — at least one owner or managerial official must complete it before enrollment and licensure.
  • Complete Provider Enrollment to be eligible for payment.
  • Background studies are completed on owners and managers; license within ninety days.

Check current DHS status before you plan

245D application availability has been subject to change at the state level. Confirm that DHS is currently accepting new applications in your service category before you commit money or a lease — we can check with you.

Checklist 05

PCA Provider

Personal Care Assistance carries its own enrollment, documentation and supervision requirements. In traditional PCA the agency hires and manages staff; in PCA Choice the client does, and the agency supports them.

  • Register your business with the Minnesota Secretary of State.
  • Obtain a Federal Tax ID and a State Tax ID. An NPI number is optional.
  • Complete the PCA Provider Application, including the name and license number of the Qualified Professional on staff.
  • Prepare a policies and procedures manual explaining your compliance with all state regulations.
  • Prepare the forms needed to document the business and the services provided to each client.

Ask us about the CFSS transition

Minnesota is moving personal care services toward Community First Services and Supports. If you are entering this space or already operating in it, talk to us about what that means for your program and your documentation before you build around the current model.
