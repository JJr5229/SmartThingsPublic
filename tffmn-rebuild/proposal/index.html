Bars show how healthy each number is — longer is better. The last row is blank on purpose. I don't have measurements from your live site, and I'm not going to invent them to make a chart look better. Running the free audit fills that row in — it takes ten minutes and costs nothing.

Requests — each one is a separate trip your visitor's phone has to make just to show the page. Fewer trips means less waiting on spotty rural wifi.

Page weight — how much data has to come down the wire. 30 KB loads on a bad connection in a parking lot; a few megabytes does not.

Third-party trackers — scripts from other companies that watch who visits. For most sites that's a privacy nuisance. For yours, given who is browsing, it's a safety question. The rebuild has none.

Security headers — instructions your site gives the browser about what it's allowed to do. They're free, and they stop whole categories of attack.

WCAG AA — the accessibility standard for text being readable. Matters for anyone with low vision, and for anyone reading on a phone in a bright car.

03 What I found

All of this comes from what Google has already indexed about your site — eleven pages, their titles, and how they're structured. No guesswork.

Your About page lives at /our-history-1High

That trailing "-1" is a leftover from a naming collision in Squarespace. It's the second most-visited page on almost every nonprofit site, and right now the link looks broken to every grant officer and journalist who sees it.

No page title targets a search anyone actually typesHigh

Every title reads "[Page] — Transforming Families MN." A frightened parent at 11pm isn't searching your name — they don't know you exist. They're searching "transgender support group Minnesota" or "my child came out what do I do." There's nothing for Google to match.

Seven communities, one pageBiggest opportunity

You run in-person groups in Minneapolis, Saint Paul, Mahtomedi, Apple Valley, Rochester, Mankato and Duluth. All of it sits on a single Meetings page. Nothing can rank for "trans youth group Mankato." Those searches are rare and enormously high-intent — they're a family looking for help within driving distance. And this is fixable without publishing a single address.

Donations are split across three platformsHigh

Givebutter, GiveMN and Benevity all carry you, with no single donate page pointing the way. Every extra decision between "I want to give" and a finished gift loses people, and split reporting makes it hard to know what's working.

No phone number anywhereWorth a conversation

Email is the only route in. For most organisations that's a minor thing. For yours it's a design question — a parent whose kid is in distress tonight doesn't want a contact form. If a staffed line isn't realistic at your size, that's completely fair, but the site should at least put 988, Trevor Project and Trans Lifeline where someone in crisis will see them. The rebuild does.

Not publishing meeting locations is the right callKeep

I want to be clear that this isn't a problem to solve away. It's a real safety control and the rebuild preserves it exactly. What it adds is a better path in its place — the page explains why there's no address, sets an expectation for how fast someone replies, and gives a one-tap email per city instead of asking the most anxious person in the room to compose a cold message to a stranger.

04 Your Google Business Profile

Most people find a local organisation on Maps before they ever see a website.

I ran seven different searches for Transforming Families — by name, by address, by "reviews," by "Maps." Your website came up. Facebook, GiveMN, GuideStar and Benevity came up. No Google Business Profile appeared, and no reviews on any platform.

That's strong evidence, not proof — Maps listings don't always show up in ordinary web search. Realistically it's one of three things: no profile was ever created, one exists but was never claimed, or one exists and has gone stale. All three have the same first step.

Start with the free audit. It scores a listing out of 100 against 22 ranking factors and shows exactly what's costing you. It's free, it takes about ten minutes, and there's no obligation attached — you can take the findings and fix them yourself.

Two things I'd want to check first: the hours currently associated with you look like the Pride Cultural Arts Center building hours, not yours — which would send people to a suite when your staff are out running groups. And a suite inside a shared building is the single most common cause of listings getting suspended, so it needs configuring carefully.
