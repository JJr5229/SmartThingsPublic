# The Prompt Library — reusable PTCF prompts for SPPS staff

Companion to Module 02. These are **starting points to adapt**, not scripts. Every one
must be edited with the participant's real context — a prompt that has not been
personalized produces generic output, which is the whole problem PTCF exists to solve.

## The rules, on every page

> **Describe the class, not the child.**
> No student names. No IEP or 504 documents. No discipline or health records. No
> gradebook exports. Verify you are in your `@spps.org` account.
> Everything below is a draft until you have reviewed it.

---

## Teachers — planning

**Unit lesson plan**
```
PERSONA: You are an experienced [grade] [subject] teacher.
TASK:    Write a [length] lesson plan introducing [topic].
CONTEXT: [N] students. [N] receive ELL support. Reading levels range roughly
         [range]. Students have iPads 1:1. This follows a unit on [prior topic]
         and leads into [next topic]. Common struggle last year: [what].
         Align to [standard].
FORMAT:  Objective, materials, 5-minute warm-up, main activity with three
         differentiated tiers in a table, exit ticket. One page.
```

**Differentiating an existing assignment**
```
PERSONA: You are an instructional coach specializing in differentiation.
TASK:    Adapt the assignment below into three versions.
CONTEXT: [grade/subject]. One version for students reading ~2 years below
         grade level, one on level, one for extension. Keep the same learning
         objective and the same final product type across all three.
         Accommodations to honor: [list the accommodations, not the documents].
FORMAT:  Three labeled versions, then a short note on what changed and why.

[paste the assignment]
```

**Leveled versions of a reading**
```
PERSONA: You are a reading specialist.
TASK:    Rewrite the passage below at three reading levels: approximately
         grades [X], [Y], [Z].
CONTEXT: [grade/subject/unit]. Preserve all key vocabulary: [list]. Keep the
         same content and the same conclusions — change accessibility only.
FORMAT:  Three labeled versions plus a shared vocabulary list with
         student-friendly definitions.
```

## Teachers — assessment

**Rubric**
```
PERSONA: You are a [subject] teacher building assessment tools.
TASK:    Write a rubric for [assignment type].
CONTEXT: [grade]. Assesses [skills]. Aligned to [standard]. Students should be
         able to self-assess with it, so language must be student-facing.
FORMAT:  4-level rubric, criteria as rows, levels as columns. Plain language.
         Describe what the work looks like, not what the student lacks.
```

**Comment bank**
```
PERSONA: You are an experienced [grade] [subject] teacher.
TASK:    Write a bank of feedback comments for [assignment type].
CONTEXT: Common issues in this class: [list, describing the class not children].
         Feedback should be specific and actionable, and name a next step.
FORMAT:  Grouped by issue. For each: what to notice, what to say, the next step
         to suggest. No praise-sandwich filler.
```

## Teachers — communication

**Family newsletter**
```
PERSONA: You are a [grade] teacher writing to families.
TASK:    Write this week's class update.
CONTEXT: We covered [topics]. Coming up: [what]. Families can support by [how].
         Reminders: [list]. Many families in our community speak languages
         other than English at home, so use plain, direct language and avoid
         idiom and educational jargon.
FORMAT:  Under 300 words. Short paragraphs, warm but efficient. Bulleted
         reminders at the end.
```
> If this will be translated: machine translation of school communication must be
> reviewed by a fluent human before sending.

**Register shift**
```
TASK:    Rewrite the message below for [audience: a family / a colleague /
         a supervisor].
CONTEXT: The purpose is [what]. The tone should be [warm / neutral / formal].
         Keep every fact unchanged.
FORMAT:  Same length or shorter.

[paste your draft]
```

## Administrators

**Meeting synthesis**
```
PERSONA: You are a chief of staff.
TASK:    Turn these raw meeting notes into a structured summary.
CONTEXT: [meeting type], attendees by role [not by name where sensitive].
FORMAT:  Decisions made · action items with owners by role · open questions ·
         items to revisit. Bulleted, one page.
```

**Report to one-page brief**
```
PERSONA: You are a district communications lead.
TASK:    Turn the report below into a one-page brief for [audience].
CONTEXT: This audience cares most about [what] and has [how much] background.
FORMAT:  Headline finding, three key points, what we're asking for, one-line
         next step.
```

**Survey summary**
```
PERSONA: You are a research analyst.
TASK:    Summarize themes in these open-ended survey responses.
CONTEXT: Survey of [population, in aggregate] about [topic]. [N] responses.
FORMAT:  Top 5 themes by frequency, each with a representative anonymized
         quote. Then dissenting or minority views — do not smooth these over.
         Then what is notably absent.
```
> Strip identifying details from responses before pasting. In a small population,
> role plus building can identify someone as surely as a name.

## Counselors and support staff

**Plain-language rewrite for families**
```
PERSONA: You are a school counselor who communicates with families.
TASK:    Rewrite the document below in plain language.
CONTEXT: Audience is families, many of whom speak languages other than English
         at home and may be unfamiliar with U.S. school systems. Target roughly
         a 6th-grade reading level. Every procedural requirement and every
         deadline must survive the rewrite exactly.
FORMAT:  Short sections with clear headings. Define every acronym on first use.
```

**Resource list**
```
PERSONA: You are a school social worker in Saint Paul, Minnesota.
TASK:    Draft a family-facing resource list for [need].
CONTEXT: For families in [area]. Include what to expect when contacting a
         service. Note where language access may be a barrier.
FORMAT:  Grouped by need. Name, what they do, how to reach them, what to expect.
```
> ⚠️ **Verify every organization, contact, and detail before sending.** AI fabricates
> plausible-looking service listings, hours, and phone numbers. This is one of the
> highest-risk uses in the entire library — a wrong number given to a family in crisis
> is a real harm. Never send this output unverified.

## Sub plans

```
PERSONA: You are a [grade] [subject] teacher writing plans for a substitute.
TASK:    Write sub plans for [N] days.
CONTEXT: We are in the middle of [unit]. Students have iPads. The sub may not
         know the subject. Class routines: [describe]. Materials available:
         [list].
FORMAT:  Per period: timing, exactly what to say, what students do, what to
         collect. Include a backup activity if things finish early.
```

## Meta-prompts — the follow-up moves

Use these on any output, from Module 02:

```
"What's weak about this? What would an instructional coach flag?"
"That tier is still too hard. Rewrite for students reading [X] years below level."
"Same content, formatted as a one-page printable handout."
"Give me three alternative approaches, then tell me which you'd pick and why."
"What am I not thinking about here?"
"Which parts of this should I verify before using?"
```

The last two are the most under-used and the most valuable — the first surfaces
blind spots, and the second turns the model into an ally in checking itself.
