# Pilot Plan — how to actually land this at SPPS

The series is written. This is the plan for getting it in front of the district without
burning the one shot you get.

---

## Phase 0 — Discovery (before anything is offered)

**Do not pitch before this is done.** Five questions from the `README.md`, plus the ones
that determine whether there is a sale at all:

**Licensing and enablement ⚠️**
1. Which Workspace for Education edition? Which add-ons?
2. Which AI features are actually enabled in the Admin console?
3. Is Gemini enabled for under-18 accounts?

**Political**
4. Who is the district AI guidance group? SPPS states administrators and district-level
   staff meet regularly on responsible AI use. **They are the buyer or the gatekeeper.**
5. Who owns staff PD budget and PowerSchool course creation?
6. Has anyone already been paid to do AI PD here? What happened?

**Content**
7. Syllabi for #90819, #90791, #90820 — so the series starts where they end.
8. Current district AI guidance for staff and students.

**How to get these answers:** ask, as a professional courtesy, before proposing anything.
"I'm putting together iPad-specific Google AI training and I want to make sure it fits
what you already run" is a conversation people take. A cold proposal is not.

## Phase 1 — The honest pitch

**Lead with the research, not the product.** The `00-district-research-brief.md` is the
credential. It demonstrates that you know SPPS runs iPads not Chromebooks, that you know
about #90819 and #90791, that you know about Minn. Stat. § 13.32. That is a different
conversation from "I do AI training."

**Lead with the gap, and be honest that it is a gap in someone else's work, not a failure
of theirs:**

> "You already teach the intro, and Google gives one away free. I'm not going to sell you
> that. What nobody has built for you is Google AI training that works on the 40,000
> iPads you actually run, and that's grounded in Minnesota data practices rather than
> just FERPA."

**Expect these objections. Have answers ready:**

| Objection | Response |
|---|---|
| "Google trains our staff free." | Correct, and participants should do it first — it's the prerequisite. It's also written for Chromebooks. |
| "We already run our own AI courses." | Yes — #90819 and #90791. This starts where those end: Gems, grounded notebooks, assessment redesign, Minnesota law. |
| "We don't have budget." | Start with the paid single-cohort pilot, not a district contract. |
| "Our lawyers handle privacy." | This is professional learning, not legal advice; district policy governs. A district rep should co-present Module 06. |
| "How do we know it works?" | Attend the Module 08 demonstrations. Judge from what staff can actually do. |

## Phase 2 — Paid pilot: one cohort

**Do not propose a district-wide rollout first.** Propose one cohort, paid, with a real
evaluation attached. Districts buy pilots far more readily than programs, and a pilot
that works sells itself internally in a way no proposal can.

**Cohort design:**
- **12–18 participants.** Small enough to circulate during hands-on blocks — which is
  where privacy problems get caught — large enough to be worth the district's time.
- **Mixed roles**, so the role tracks in Module 08 are real. Include at least one
  administrator and one support-staff member; both groups are chronically underserved by
  teacher-shaped PD, and the administrator use cases are the most persuasive.
- **Volunteers, not conscripts.** A mandated cohort evaluates badly regardless of quality.
- **Include one skeptic deliberately.** A skeptic who is won over is the best internal
  advocate available, and their objections improve the material.

**Schedule options:**
- Eight weekly 90-minute sessions — best retention, hardest to schedule
- Four half-days — most common fit for district PD calendars
- Two full days + async — fastest, weakest retention; needs a mandatory 30-day follow-up

**Non-negotiables:**
- A district representative attends or co-presents **Module 06**
- Every participant completes the free prerequisite course first
- **A 30-day follow-up session is part of the engagement, not an add-on.** Practice
  decays without it, and the follow-up is where the evaluation data comes from.

## Phase 3 — Evaluation

Measure things a district can act on, not satisfaction scores.

**At Module 08:** the artifact audit from the capstone — how many of seven artifacts are
in actual use, by role. This is real data and it is honest, including when it is
unflattering.

**At the 30-day follow-up:**
- Which workflows are still running?
- Estimated hours saved per week, self-reported
- What broke, and why
- How many participants taught something to a colleague?

**The credibility move:** report the failures too. A pilot report saying "Module 03 landed
poorly because three of the features weren't enabled for our license, here's the revised
version" builds far more trust than a report claiming everything worked. Districts have
seen the everything-worked report before and discount it.

## Phase 4 — Scale

If the pilot works, the natural next steps, in order of what districts actually buy:

1. **Train-the-trainer cohort** — Module 08 Track D, building coaches who deliver
   internally. This is what a district wants long-term and it is an honest thing to sell,
   even though it reduces your delivery revenue. Say so; it builds the relationship.
2. **Role-specific short courses** — the administrator track and the counselor/support
   track as standalone half-days. Both groups are underserved.
3. **Annual refresh** — genuinely necessary, not manufactured. Google shipped multiple
   material changes to education AI during 2026 alone.
4. **Curriculum licensing** — the district runs it themselves through PowerSchool with
   your materials.

## A note on the risk you are taking

The honest assessment: this is a real opportunity with a real chance of not converting.

**In favor:** SPPS already spends on AI PD, so the category is proven internally. The
iPad gap is genuine and unaddressed. The Minnesota law angle is genuinely absent from
national training. You have done research nobody else pitching them will have done.

**Against:** the district may consider itself already served by its own courses plus
Google's free ones. Budget cycles are slow. Districts are cautious about outside vendors
touching anything privacy-adjacent. And Google's free offering keeps getting better.

**The realistic path is the small paid pilot**, evaluated honestly, that turns into
something larger — not a district contract from a cold proposal. Plan for the former and
you will not waste the opportunity chasing the latter.
