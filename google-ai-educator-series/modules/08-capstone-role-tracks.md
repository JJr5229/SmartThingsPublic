# Module 08 — Capstone: Role Tracks & Your Toolkit

**Time:** 120 minutes
**Prerequisite:** Modules 01–07
**Artifact produced:** Published personal toolkit + peer demonstration

---

## Why this module exists

Seven modules have produced seven artifacts. Without this one, they stay scattered across
a Drive folder and are gone by November.

The capstone does three things: it **consolidates** the artifacts into one usable
toolkit, it **differentiates by role** for the first time (everything so far has been
common), and it **creates the accountability of a public demonstration** — which is what
actually converts training into practice.

It is also, frankly, the district's evidence that the money produced something. The peer
demonstrations are the deliverable a director can attend.

## Objectives

By the end, participants can:

1. Consolidate their work into a coherent, maintainable personal toolkit
2. Demonstrate one workflow to peers well enough that a colleague can adopt it
3. Commit to specific practice with named next steps and a maintenance schedule
4. Identify what they will teach others in their building

---

## Facilitator script

### 1. Inventory (15 min)

Participants collect the seven artifacts:

| Module | Artifact |
|---|---|
| 01 | Tool-boundary map |
| 02 | 5-prompt starter library |
| 03 | Three weekly workflows |
| 04 | Course notebook + Audio Overview |
| 05 | A working Gem |
| 06 | Team data-handling rule |
| 07 | Redesigned assessment |

Then the honest audit — ask each participant to mark each artifact:

- **Used it since building it** ✅
- **Meant to, haven't** ⚠️
- **Won't use it** ❌

**Do not skip or soften this.** The ❌ items are the most informative thing in the room —
for the participant, and for improving the series. Ask what made those fail. The answers
are usually specific and fixable: the feature wasn't licensed, the workflow didn't fit
the week, the Gem was too vague.

### 2. Role tracks (40 min)

Split the room. First differentiation in the series.

#### Track A — Classroom teachers
Focus: planning, differentiation, feedback, and assessment at unit scale.
- Extend the Gem from Module 05 to a full unit or course assistant
- Build the second and third notebooks; establish the one-per-unit habit
- Build a **differentiation pipeline**: one core text → leveled versions → tiered tasks →
  aligned exit tickets, as a repeatable sequence rather than a one-off
- Set the traffic-light expectation for every assessment in the next unit

#### Track B — Administrators and district staff
Focus: communication, synthesis, and policy work at volume.
- Build the **grounded policy notebook** — handbooks, board policies, procedures — that
  answers "what is our actual policy on X?" *with citations*. This is the single highest-
  value artifact for this group.
- Workflows for staff communications, meeting synthesis, and survey summarization
- Turning long reports into one-page briefs for different audiences
- **Modeling responsible use publicly** — administrators set the norm. If leadership uses
  AI carelessly, no policy survives it.

#### Track C — Counselors, social workers, specialists, support staff
Focus: documentation, resources, and communication, under the strictest data constraints.
- Resource and procedure notebooks
- Drafting and register-shifting for family communication
- **The hardest de-identification cases in the district.** This group handles the most
  sensitive data and gets the least training targeted at them. Give the drill extra time
  here and use their real scenarios.
- Plain-language rewriting of dense documents for families

#### Track D — Instructional coaches and building leads
Focus: spreading this beyond the room.
- Build the coaching protocol for supporting colleagues
- Identify the two or three highest-value practices for their building
- Plan a building-level share-out
- **This track is how the district gets scale**, and it is the natural upsell: a
  train-the-trainer cohort.

### 3. Peer demonstrations (40 min)

Each participant demonstrates **one workflow** in five minutes:

1. What problem it solves and how long it used to take
2. Live demonstration
3. The review step and the data rule that governs it
4. What they got wrong the first time

The fourth item is required. A room that only sees polished successes learns much less
than one that hears "my first Gem was too vague and produced garbage until I added the
pacing guide."

**Invite district leadership to this block.** It is the most persuasive 40 minutes in the
series, and it is the natural moment for a conversation about the next cohort.

### 4. Commitments and maintenance (15 min)

Practice decays without maintenance. Build the schedule explicitly:

- **Weekly:** run the three workflows from Module 03
- **Per unit:** new notebook; update the Gem; set assessment expectations
- **Quarterly:** review the team data rule against current district guidance
- **Annually:** re-verify everything — Google changes these products constantly

### 5. What comes next (10 min)

Be honest about limits. This series covers the Google stack as it exists now. It does
not cover: Microsoft Copilot (which SPPS also has cause to use), Canva Magic Studio
(the district trains this separately, #90820), or the many third-party education AI
tools staff will encounter.

**And the products will change.** Several features taught here shifted during 2026 alone.
The durable skills are PTCF prompting, source grounding, de-identification, and
assessment design — those transfer to whatever ships next. Say this; it is true, and it
sets up the refresh engagement honestly rather than by manufactured urgency.

## The artifact — published toolkit

One Google Doc or Site, in the participant's own Drive, organized to be *used* rather
than filed:

```
MY GOOGLE AI TOOLKIT — [name, role, building]

MY ACCOUNT & TOOLS: [from Module 01]
MY DATA RULE: [from Module 06 — first, deliberately]

PROMPT LIBRARY: [Module 02, expanded]
WEEKLY WORKFLOWS: [Module 03 — with times saved]
MY NOTEBOOKS: [Module 04 — links, one per unit]
MY GEMS: [Module 05 — purpose and knowledge for each]
ASSESSMENT APPROACH: [Module 07 — traffic-light language]

MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE: weekly / per-unit / quarterly / annual
WHAT I'LL TEACH A COLLEAGUE: ______
WHEN I'M UNSURE, I ASK: ______
```

The data rule sits at the top on purpose. It should be the first thing seen on every
open.

## Pitfalls

**Toolkit as scrapbook.** A pretty document nobody opens. Force the organization to be
task-based — "what do I do on Friday" — not module-based.

**Demonstration anxiety.** Some participants dread presenting. Offer a small-group
option. The demonstration matters; the audience size does not.

**The honest audit hurts.** If a participant marks four of seven artifacts ❌, the
temptation is to reassure them. Better to get specific about why. Usually it is
licensing, workload, or a vague artifact — all fixable, and all useful feedback for the
next cohort.

**Overcommitment.** Participants leave enthusiastic and plan six new practices. Hold them
to **two or three.** A teacher who reliably runs three workflows every week is far ahead
of one who planned nine and does none.

**No follow-up.** The largest risk to the whole series. Without a check-in, practice
decays. Build a 30-day follow-up into the engagement — see `facilitation/pilot-plan.md`.

## Check for understanding

The demonstration *is* the assessment. The criterion:

> Could a colleague who missed the series adopt this workflow from your five minutes —
> including knowing what to review and what never to put in it?

If yes, the participant has genuinely internalized the material. If the data rule and
review step are missing from the demonstration, they have not, regardless of how good
the workflow is.

## iPad notes

- Toolkits should be **built on a MacBook and used on iPad**. Confirm each participant's
  toolkit actually opens and functions on their iPad before they leave.
- Demonstrations should run on the device the participant actually uses in front of
  students. A laptop-only demo hides exactly the friction the next person will hit.
