# Module 03 — Gemini Across Workspace (iPad-first)

**Time:** 90 minutes
**Prerequisite:** Modules 01–02
**Artifact produced:** Three automated weekly workflows

---

## Why this module exists

The intro courses demo Gemini in a browser tab. Real time savings come from Gemini
*where the work already is* — in the document you are writing, the inbox you are
answering, the slides you are building at 10pm.

And this is precisely where the iPad problem bites hardest. Every published walkthrough
of "Gemini in Docs" assumes desktop Chrome with a right-hand side panel. On a district
iPad the entry points are somewhere else or absent. **This module is the one that
justifies the whole series' existence**, because nobody else has written it for these
devices.

## Objectives

By the end, participants can:

1. Invoke Gemini inside Docs, Gmail, Slides, and Sheets — on both iPad and MacBook
2. Identify three recurring weekly tasks and convert each to a Gemini-assisted workflow
3. Know which Workspace AI features their edition actually includes
4. Judge when Gemini in Workspace is the wrong tool

## What you need enabled ⚠️

**This is the most licensing-sensitive module in the series.** Gemini in Docs, Sheets,
Slides, Vids, and Forms is tied to **Education Plus** (or a Teaching & Learning add-on
for select capabilities, users 18+), or a Google AI Pro for Education add-on.

**If SPPS holds Education Fundamentals with no add-on, most of this module cannot be
delivered as written.** Do not discover this in the room.

**Contingency:** if in-app Gemini is unavailable, run the module against the standalone
Gemini app with copy-paste workflows. Slower, but the *workflow design* skill — which is
the actual objective — transfers intact. Prepare this variant before every delivery.

## Facilitator script

### 1. Confirm the surface, live (10 min)

Before teaching anything, have every participant open a Google Doc on their own device
and find Gemini. Do this as a group with hands raised, and **record what people actually
see** — it will vary by device and possibly by license.

This is deliberately the first activity because it surfaces breakage immediately, while
you can still adapt, rather than 40 minutes in.

### 2. Gemini in Docs (20 min)

The highest-value surface for most educators. Demo three moves:

**Draft from a prompt.** "Help me write" with a PTCF prompt from Module 02's library.
Point out that the library is now paying off — they are pasting, not composing.

**Refine existing text.** Select a paragraph → rewrite for tone, length, or reading
level. This is the single most useful move for staff: taking a message they already
wrote and adapting it for a different audience.

**The reading-level move.** Take one piece of real content and generate three versions
at different reading levels. In a district with substantial ELL enrollment this is
immediately, obviously valuable — lead with it if the room needs convincing.

### 3. Gemini in Gmail (20 min)

Where administrators and support staff get their hours back.

- **Summarize a long thread** before replying — especially a 30-message parent thread
- **Draft a reply** from a short instruction, then refine tone
- **Adjust register:** the same message to a colleague, to a family, to a supervisor

**Teach the family-communication caution here, hard.** AI-drafted messages to families
carry real risk: they can be subtly wrong about a student, land in the wrong register,
or read as impersonal about something that matters enormously to the person reading it.

The rule: *Gemini drafts the structure; you write the parts about the child.* A message
about a specific student's specific situation must contain your own sentences about that
student. Demonstrate a bad example — a warm, fluent, generic AI paragraph about a
struggling reader — and ask the room how a parent would feel receiving it. The point
lands better as a question than as a rule.

### 4. Slides and Sheets (15 min)

**Slides:** generate a deck outline from a doc or prompt; generate images for slides.
Set expectations honestly — AI-generated decks need real design work, and the district
also trains staff on **Canva Magic Studio** (PowerSchool #90820), which many staff will
prefer for visual polish. Say so; it builds trust and it is true.

**Sheets:** help with formulas ("write a formula that flags any score below 70"),
generate table structures, summarize data ranges. For most classroom teachers this is
the least-used surface; for administrators and data-facing staff it is the most.

⚠️ **Never paste an identifiable student data set into any AI tool** to "analyze it."
Flag it here and formalize it in Module 06.

### 5. Build the workflows (25 min)

## Hands-on block

Each participant identifies **three tasks they do every single week** and builds a
Gemini-assisted version of each — using their real material, in their real account,
finishing with a real usable output.

Prompt with role-specific examples:

*Teachers:* weekly family newsletter · differentiating an existing assignment ·
converting a reading into leveled versions · drafting feedback comment banks ·
sub plans

*Administrators:* meeting agendas from notes · summarizing staff survey responses ·
drafting staff communications · turning a long report into a one-page brief

*Counselors / support staff:* summarizing documentation you wrote · drafting resource
lists for families · translating a message into plainer language

**Requirement: they finish with real output they will actually send or use this week.**
A workflow that is not used within seven days is never used. Say that out loud.

## The artifact — three weekly workflows

```
WORKFLOW: [name]
FREQUENCY: [every Friday / start of each unit]
WHERE: [Docs / Gmail / Slides / Sheets / Gemini app]
TIME BEFORE: ___ min      TIME AFTER: ___ min

THE PROMPT: [PTCF, saved in my prompt library]
MY REVIEW STEP: [what I always check before this goes out]
NEVER INCLUDE: [student names / IEP details / discipline records]
```

The **review step** is required, not optional. A workflow without a named human review
step is a workflow that will eventually send something wrong to a family.

## Pitfalls

**The licensing cliff.** See above. The most likely way this module fails.

**Feature drift.** Google ships Workspace AI changes constantly — several capabilities
cited here changed during 2026. **Re-verify every demo the week before delivery.**
A facilitator confidently demoing a moved button loses the room.

**Slides disappointment.** AI-generated slides consistently underwhelm relative to
expectations. Set the expectation *before* the demo, not after.

**"This is faster to do myself."** Sometimes true, and conceding it builds enormous
credibility. The honest framing: Gemini wins on **volume, variation, and first drafts**,
loses on **short tasks you have already automated in your head.** A teacher who writes a
family email in four minutes should not use AI for it. One who needs the same email in
three reading levels and two languages should.

**Silent wrong-account use.** During hands-on, participants reach for whatever is signed
in. Circulate. Check avatars.

## Check for understanding

Participants demonstrate one workflow to a partner, who must be able to answer:
*What is the review step, and what must never go into this prompt?*

If the partner cannot answer both, the workflow is not finished.

## iPad notes

**This section is the module's real value — invest the preparation time.**

- **Verify and document the actual iPad entry points for every surface before delivery.**
  They differ from the desktop side panel and they move between app versions. Screenshot
  them fresh each cycle. This documentation is a genuine deliverable to the district.
- Some Workspace Gemini features may be **web-only** and effectively unavailable in the
  iPad apps. Where that is true, teach the Safari-on-iPad path with "Request Desktop
  Site" as the fallback, and be candid that it is a workaround.
- **Split View / Slide Over** — Docs beside Gemini — is the core iPad productivity move
  for this module. Most staff have never used it. Teach it explicitly and slowly.
- **Dictation** for long prompts, again.
- Staff MacBooks get the full desktop experience; student iPads do not. If staff are
  building things students will use, they must test on an iPad. Make this a rule.
