# The Everyday Claude Guides — Curriculum Spec
*What each book actually teaches, lesson by lesson. Companion to `everyday-claude-roadmap.md`, which covers positioning and channels.*

Last updated: 2026-09-03

---

## The unit of teaching

**A lesson is one teachable idea.** It runs 2–4 pages and always has the same four beats:

1. **The friction**, named in one line — the thing the reader already feels.
2. **The idea** — what to do instead. One idea. Never two.
3. **One real example, shown in full** — the actual words typed, the actual answer back. Never summarized, never "you might say something like."
4. **Try this now** — one concrete action, doable in under two minutes.

Lessons are numbered and titled as plain statements, not clever headings. The reader should be able to find a lesson again a week later by remembering roughly what it was about.

## The book spine

Every book in the line uses the same skeleton, so a reader who finishes one already knows how to read the next:

| Part | Pages | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Cover | 1 | Ember gradient, shared identity |
| You are here | 1 | The promise, as plain outcomes |
| Lessons | 2–4 each | The body |
| The artifact | 1–4 | The copy-paste-ready thing (see each book) |
| When it goes wrong | 1–2 | Troubleshooting, in the reader's language |
| Current as of [quarter] | 1 | The quarantined version-specific facts |
| If you only do one thing | 1 | Permission to stop. Names the single highest-value lesson |
| Do this next | 1 | Cross-links |
| Disclaimer | 1 | Required, verbatim |

Front and back matter runs ~8 pages. So: **8 lessons ≈ 32 pages, 10 lessons ≈ 38 pages.**

## Two rules that shape every lesson in the line

- **No bracketed fill-in-the-blanks.** Every example ask is a complete sentence the reader can type word for word. Where they need to substitute their own details, the guide shows a second, different complete version rather than a template with slots.
- **Every book says once, plainly, that Claude can be confidently wrong.** Not buried in a caveat — as its own lesson or its own page, in the body.

# Tier 0 — Free

## "Your First Ten Minutes With Claude"

**6 pages. Not lessons — one path.** This is not a small book; it's a single guided experience. Enormous type. One decision the whole way through.

The reader ends with one genuinely good result they'd show someone. That's the entire success criterion.

| Page | What's on it |
|---|---|
| 1 | **You're not late.** No instruction. Just the reassurance, and a promise that this takes ten minutes and costs nothing. |
| 2 | **Where to go, and what you'll see.** One address. A description of the screen in ordinary words — "a big empty box, near the middle, with a cursor blinking in it." |
| 3 | **Type this.** The one first ask, printed large enough to copy from across a table. |
| 4 | **What just happened.** Why that worked, in four sentences. This is the page that converts a novelty into a skill. |
| 5 | **Now do it with your own.** The same move applied to something of theirs. |
| 6 | **If that was useful.** One link, one email signup, three titles named. |

**The first ask** — this choice matters more than anything else in the free guide, because it's the entire product:

> I need to send a message to someone and I'm not sure how to word it. It's a bit awkward. Ask me a few questions about the situation first, then help me write it.

Chosen deliberately over "write me a poem" or "plan my week." It is universal, it carries real feeling, the output is obviously better than what the reader would have written alone, and the question-asking makes Claude feel like a collaborator rather than a vending machine in the first thirty seconds.

**Backup ask** on page 5 for readers who don't have an awkward message pending:

> I got a letter I don't fully understand. I'm going to paste it in. Explain what it actually says and whether I need to do anything.

## "The No-Jargon Claude Glossary"

**15 entries, 4 clusters, 13 pages · BUILT.** One term per page for the first cluster, two per page after.

**Cluster 1 — The words for the thing itself**
1. AI — what the word actually means here, stripped of hype
2. Claude — the assistant this whole series is about
3. Anthropic — the company that makes it

**Cluster 2 — The words for what you do**
4. Prompt — your message. Not a magic phrase.
5. Chat / conversation — one session, start to finish
6. Upload / attach — showing it a document or photo
7. Model — the engine answering. Why there's more than one.

**Cluster 3 — The words for how it behaves**
8. Context — what it can hold in mind at once
9. Memory — why it forgets, and when it doesn't
10. Hallucination — making something up while sounding certain. **The most important entry in the book.**

**Cluster 4 — The words you'll see in the app**
11. Project — a workspace that already knows your situation
12. Custom instructions — standing rules you set once
13. Artifact — a document it makes alongside the chat
14. Connector — a link to another app you use
15. Free / Pro / Max — the plan names

**Closing page: "Words you can safely ignore."** Lists the terms this reader will encounter and does *not* need — LLM, token, MCP, API, Skill, Claude Code, prompt engineering, fine-tuning — with one line saying these are for developers and hearing them is not a sign you're behind. This page does more for the reader's confidence than the fifteen definitions do.

# Tier 1 — Starting out

## No. 1 — Claude for Absolute Beginners
**10 lessons · 38 pages · BUILT · the anchor title**

*Promise: you'll have an account, a first real conversation behind you, and a clear sense of what this is good for — without anyone explaining a single thing you didn't ask about.*

| # | Lesson | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | You are not late to this | The reframe. Two years of hearing about AI has taught them nothing except that they're behind. Undo that before teaching anything. |
| 2 | It's a conversation, not a search box | The single most load-bearing idea in the book. Search rewards three words; this rewards explaining yourself. |
| 3 | Getting in | Account, free plan, website vs. app, phone vs. computer. Framed as "five minutes, no installation." |
| 4 | What you're looking at | The screen, described in ordinary words. Where the box is. Where old conversations go. How to start a new one. |
| 5 | Your first real conversation | Guided, start to finish, with the full exchange printed. |
| 6 | Tell it what it can't guess | Context, taught as a habit, not a technique. It doesn't know your situation until you say so. |
| 7 | The answer's close — now fix it | Refining. "Shorter." "Warmer." "Try that again but assume I've never done this." |
| 8 | Why it forgets, and what to do about it | New chat vs. carry on. Prevents the most common beginner frustration. |
| 9 | Sometimes it's wrong, and it won't sound wrong | The honesty lesson. Required in every book; this is its home. |
| 10 | Where this fits in your week | Closing. Three realistic slots, not a productivity system. |

**Artifact — The First Week Card.** One page, printable, seven small things to try, one per day. Day 1 is the conversation from lesson 5; day 7 is repeating whichever one worked. Designed to be torn out and left next to the laptop.

## No. 4 — Asking Better Questions
**8 lessons · 31 pages · BUILT**

*Promise: you'll get a useful answer the first time instead of a vague one you keep having to fix.*

This is the consumer counterpart to the business line's prompting guide — same substance, taught as habits. **No acronym, no framework diagram, no letters to memorize.**

| # | Lesson | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The answer was boring because the question was | Diagnosis before instruction. Shows the same bad ask and good ask side by side, before explaining why. |
| 2 | Say who it's for | The largest single lever. "Write a birthday message" vs. "Write a birthday message for my father-in-law, who I don't know well." |
| 3 | Say what you actually want | Verb and outcome. Not "help me with" — "write," "explain," "shorten," "compare." |
| 4 | Say what good looks like | Length, tone, shape. And the strongest move available: show it one example you liked. |
| 5 | Tell it the things only you know | Context without dumping. What's relevant, what isn't. |
| 6 | Ask for three, not one | Options beat answers. Removes the pressure to get the ask perfect. |
| 7 | Nudge it, don't start over | The refining phrases, listed. The most time-saving lesson in the book. |
| 8 | Let it ask you | The reverse move. "Ask me some questions first, then help." Works when the reader doesn't know what they want yet. |

**Artifact — The Ask Builder.** Not a template with blanks. Four questions the reader answers on paper — *Who's it for? What do you want? How long and what tone? What do you know that it doesn't?* — and their four written answers, read out in order, **are** the message. Teaches the structure by having them produce one, without a single bracket.

**Plus: 12 before-and-after pairs** from ordinary life — the school email, the contractor complaint, the recipe substitution, the thank-you note. Both versions printed in full.

## No. 3 — 101 Everyday Things to Ask Claude
**Not a lesson book — a catalog · 26 pages · BUILT · the volume leader**

*Promise: open to any page, find something worth trying today.*

Two pages of front matter (how to use this, and how to change one to fit you), then ten chapters of ten to eleven asks each. **Every ask is a complete sentence, printed ready to type.** No brackets, no slots.

| Chapter | Sample of what's in it |
|---|---|
| Around the house | Fixing, cleaning, sorting, "what is this thing" |
| Food and shopping | Meals from what's in the fridge, lists, substitutions |
| Messages I don't want to write | The complaint, the no, the condolence, the chase |
| Making sense of confusing things | Letters, instructions, terms, forms |
| Money and paperwork | Understanding a bill, comparing options — never advice |
| Doctor's appointments | Preparing questions, understanding what you were told |
| Family and kids | Homework help that teaches, activities, hard conversations |
| Going places | Trips, outings, logistics |
| Learning something | Languages, hobbies, skills, practice |
| When you're stuck | Overwhelmed, deciding, can't start |

**Design note:** this is the browse-and-flip book. It should survive being opened at random, so no ask depends on having read a previous page.

## No. 2 — Is This Safe?
**9 lessons · 29 pages · BUILT · the trust product**

*Promise: you'll know what it can see, what it can't, when it's likely to be wrong, and the handful of things you should never hand it.*

This is a trust product as much as an instruction product. For an audience that is cautious by default, it's the book that makes every other book in the line safe to sell — and lesson 8 is content no competing beginner guide is writing.

| # | Lesson | What it teaches |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The three things people worry about | Names them out loud: is it stealing my information, is it lying to me, am I being replaced. Sorts real from imagined. |
| 2 | What it can and can't see | It does not read your email, your files, or your photos unless you hand them over. Removes the biggest unspoken fear. |
| 3 | What happens to what you type | Plain-language, honest, and points the reader at where to check the current answer themselves rather than asserting one. |
| 4 | Four things to never type in | Passwords, card and account numbers, government ID numbers, other people's private details. Short, blunt, memorable. |
| 5 | Why it sometimes makes things up | Confidence is not accuracy. Explains *why* this happens in one page, without technical vocabulary. |
| 6 | How to catch it | The three-question check, applied to a real wrong answer shown in full. |
| 7 | The four things you always confirm with a person | Money, law, health, and anything you can't undo. This is the standing rule the whole line inherits. |
| 8 | Scams that use this stuff — and how not to be the mark | Voice cloning on a phone call from "your grandson," too-fluent emails, fake urgency. **The highest-value pages in the entire line for older readers, and nobody else is writing them.** |
| 9 | A sane way to use it | Closing posture: free-hand for drafting and understanding, careful for deciding and sending. |

**Artifact — The Double-Check Card.** Three questions to ask about any answer before acting on it: *Could I verify this in one search? Does it matter if it's wrong? Would I want a person to confirm it?* Printable, one page.

# Build order

**Catalog numbers now follow ship order** (renumbered 2026-09-03) so the shelf has no gaps —
a customer seeing No. 1 and No. 4 with nothing between them reads as an incomplete series.

1. ✅ **Your First Ten Minutes** — free, 6 pages. Locked the type size, voice and look for the line.
2. ✅ **No. 1 — Claude for Absolute Beginners** — 38 pages. The anchor; everything cross-links back to it.
3. ✅ **No. 2 — Is This Safe?** — 29 pages. The trust product. Lesson 8 (AI-enabled scams) is the line's most distinctive content.
4. ✅ **No. 3 — 101 Everyday Things** — 26 pages, exactly 101 asks, verified by count at build time.
5. ✅ **The No-Jargon Glossary** — free, 13 pages. The closing "words you can safely ignore" page is the point as much as the definitions.
6. ✅ **No. 4 — Asking Better Questions** — 31 pages, 12 before-and-after pairs.

7. ✅ **No. 5 — Letters, Emails, and Awkward Messages** — 38 pages, 12 finished messages. First Tier 2 title.

8. ✅ **No. 6 — Around the House** — 25 pages, plus a month of kitchen asks for the fridge.
9. ✅ **No. 7 — Family Life** — 26 pages, plus the homework helper card.

**Nine products built: two free, seven paid.** Tier 2 has two left: *Paperwork and Plain English* and *Learning Something New*.

**Production note.** All books share one builder and one stylesheet at
`veer/everyday-claude-guides/build/`. Each guide folder holds `build.json` and
`Guide/manuscript.md`; run `python3 build/build_guide.py "guides/<Name>"`. The build **fails**
if any page would be clipped, and reports pages under half full. *Your First Ten Minutes* keeps
its own bespoke build — it's a 6-page single-path layout, not the standard book spine.

That's a coherent six-product line, one free entry point, and a bundle worth selling before a single Tier 2 book exists.
