### The Everyday Claude Guides · No. 3 · by Veer

*Ten chapters. 101 complete sentences you can type word for word.*

**Maintenance note:** this book contains **no version-specific facts** — no model names, plan
names, prices or menu labels — so it has no `PAGE current`. Nothing in it can go stale. Keep it
that way; if a version fact ever needs to appear, add a current page rather than burying it.

**Format rule:** every ask is a complete sentence with nothing to fill in. No brackets, no slots.

"I'll describe it" rather than leaving a gap.

## PAGE plain | How to use this

This isn't a book to read. It's a book to open at random when you're not sure what to do with this thing.

**Every line is ready to type.** Nothing to fill in, nothing to work out first. Type it, or say it, exactly as written.

**Change anything you like.** These are starting points, not spells. Swapping a detail for your own makes them better, not broken.

**Most of them expect a conversation.** Many will ask you a question back. That's deliberate — the answer gets much better once it knows your situation.

**If one doesn't work, say so.** *That's not quite it, what I actually meant was…* is a normal part of using this, not a sign you did it wrong.

## PAGE lesson 01 | Around the house

```asks | Type any of these
My washing machine is making a grinding noise during the spin cycle. Walk me through what to check before I call someone out.
I'm going to describe a stain and what fabric it's on, and I'd like the best way to get it out.
Give me a realistic cleaning routine for a small flat that takes twenty minutes a day.
I have a cupboard full of half-used cleaning products. Tell me which ones I actually need and which are duplicates.
Explain how to bleed a radiator, one step at a time, as if I've never held a spanner.
```

## PAGE plain | When one doesn't work

Occasionally you'll type one of these and get something flat back. Almost always it's one of three things, and all three take seconds to fix.

**It didn't know enough about your situation.** These sentences are deliberately general so they fit everybody. Add a line about your circumstances and ask again in the same conversation.

**It gave you too much.** Say *shorter*, or *give me three options rather than an essay*, or *a list, not paragraphs*.

**It answered a slightly different question.** Say *that's not quite what I meant* and put it in your own words. You don't need to start over.

## PAGE plain | If you only do one thing

Take one line from this book and leave the other hundred.

**"Ask me five questions that would help me think this through, then wait for my answers."**

It works on almost anything — a decision, a problem, a task you're avoiding, a conversation you're dreading. It flips the thing from something that hands you an answer into something that helps you find your own. And it's the line people report coming back to long after they've forgotten the rest.

If nothing else in this book ever gets used, that was worth it.
